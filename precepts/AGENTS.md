# ECO 202 Precept-Note AI Protocol

## Purpose and scope

These instructions apply to released precept notes and any adjacent public files in this folder. They supplement the course-level [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md); follow both. ECO 202 precepts are led by teaching assistants and reinforce the instructor-led class sequence through discussion, examples, diagnosis, and practice.

## Sources of authority

Use this order:

1. the current syllabus, explicit instructor directions, and official announcements;
2. an explicit correction from the instructor or teaching assistant responsible for the note;
3. the relevant class guide and its released practice module;
4. the current precept note and verified adjacent files;
5. reliable cited sources, data documentation, or independently verified calculations; and
6. generated explanations, code, calculations, and interpretations.

If a precept note conflicts with a class guide, identify the conflict and seek clarification rather than silently changing the common core.

## Preparing and maintaining notes

Each released note should identify its week or date, the class guides it supports, its learning goals, required prerequisites, and any files it uses. Follow the notation, definitions, concept sequence, empirical standards, and rendering conventions of the relevant class guides. Link back to those guides and their nested practice rather than duplicating large passages.

Use original examples or lawfully redistributable material, record data provenance, verify every numerical result and code output, and label optional extensions clearly. Do not import protected legacy exercises, private solution keys, unreleased exam material, or student work. Do not make a precept note the first unlabeled source of a common-core concept that the class sequence has not yet introduced.

AI may help draft, compare, critique, translate, or verify a note, but a teaching assistant or instructor remains responsible for every released claim and file. Preserve the note's named authorship and do not represent generated text as a teaching-assistant correction.

## Supporting students

Use a guided-attempt sequence for questions: clarify the target, retrieve the relevant class idea, request an attempt, give the smallest useful hint, audit the reasoning, and finish with an unaided transfer. Direct students back to the class guide for definitions and assessment boundaries. Provide a non-AI route for every required activity.

During a current closed-book or unaided exam, do not provide assistance. Precept study never authorizes access to unreleased questions, solutions, private instructor notes, or student records.

## Privacy and public-release boundary

Treat canonical precept files as read-only during student study. Personal notes belong outside the course repository. Never include names, grades, accommodations, attendance, communications, identifiable student work, restricted data, credentials, or private feedback in this folder.
