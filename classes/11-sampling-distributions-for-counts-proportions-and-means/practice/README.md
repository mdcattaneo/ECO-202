## Purpose and workload

This practice keeps the population, sampling mechanism, and statistic visible while moving from one sample to a distribution over repeated samples. You will classify the statistical objects, enumerate a small sampling distribution, derive exact centers and spreads for counts, proportions, and means, verify the square-root sample-size pattern in the fixed `wage1` teaching population, and audit what a simulation does and does not establish.

Problems 1–6 form an exact 40–55 minute core under the stage ranges below. Complete Problems 1–5 without AI, statistical software, compact checks, or worked solutions; an ordinary calculator is fine. Problem 6 includes a complete non-AI audit and final unaided transfer in the timed core. Its optional AI comparison, answer-check study, worked-solution study, and optional extensions take additional time.

## What you must be able to do independently

After completing the core route, you should be able to:

- distinguish a fixed population distribution, one realized sample distribution, a statistic before sampling, one realized statistic, and a sampling distribution;
- state which objects stay fixed and which change when the stated sampling mechanism is repeated;
- derive exact centers and standard errors for a sample mean, count, and sample proportion under independent sampling with replacement;
- track the different scales and units of counts, proportions, observations, and sample means;
- explain and calculate the $1/\sqrt n$ standard-error pattern without making a distributional-shape claim;
- compare an analytic standard error with the empirical standard deviation from a repeated-sampling simulation; and
- audit claims about realized error, duplicate rows, simulation discrepancy, population coverage, and distributional shape.

### Problem 1. Population, sample, statistic, or distribution?

**Class-guide connection:** [A statistic has a distribution](../#c11-stop-1) and [One sample is one realization](../#c11-stop-2)

A company has a fixed register containing the dollar values of 500 invoices. A teaching experiment selects 16 register rows independently and uniformly with replacement and calculates the sample mean invoice value. One realized sample has mean 84.50 dollars.

1. Identify the population, observational unit, variable, sampling mechanism, and statistic.
2. Classify each object as a population distribution, one realized sample distribution, a statistic before sampling, one realized statistic, or a sampling distribution: all 500 invoice values; the 16 selected values; the rule $\overline X_{16}=16^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{16}X_i$ before selection; the number 84.50; and the distribution of $\overline X_{16}$ over all possible ordered samples.
3. Suppose the experiment is repeated. State which of the following can change and which remain fixed: the 16 selected rows, the rule $\overline X_{16}$, the realized sample-mean value (84.50 in the original sample), the 500-row distribution, the population mean, and the sampling distribution of $\overline X_{16}$.
4. Explain why neither the 16 observed values nor the one number 84.50 is the sampling distribution.

### Problem 2. Enumerate every possible sample mean

**Class-guide connection:** [A statistic has a distribution](../#c11-stop-1)

A two-record teaching population contains payment values 3 and 9, measured in tens of dollars. Select two records independently and uniformly with replacement and calculate $\overline Y_2$.

1. List all four equally likely ordered samples and calculate $\overline Y_2$ for each.
2. Construct the sampling distribution of $\overline Y_2$ and verify that its probabilities sum to one.
3. Calculate $\mathbb E[\overline Y_2]$, $\mathrm{Var}(\overline Y_2)$, and $\mathrm{SD}(\overline Y_2)$ directly from that distribution.
4. Calculate the population mean $\mu_Y$ and population variance $\sigma_Y^2$ of one uniform draw. Verify that $\mathbb E[\overline Y_2]=\mu_Y$ and $\mathrm{Var}(\overline Y_2)=\sigma_Y^2/2$.
5. If the realized ordered sample is $(3,9)$, identify the realized sample distribution and realized sample mean. Explain which full sampling-distribution quantities do not change after observing it.

### Problem 3. Counts and proportions from indicators

**Class-guide connection:** [Counts and proportions from indicators](../#c11-stop-4)

Under an explicitly stated independent with-replacement sampling model, let $H_i=1$ when draw $i$ has a specified characteristic and $H_i=0$ otherwise, with $\mathbb P(H_i=1)=p=0.36$. Define

$$
\begin{aligned}
C_n &= \sum_{i=1}^n H_i, \\
\widehat{P}_n &= \frac{C_n}{n}.
\end{aligned}
$$

1. Starting from the indicator mean and variance, derive $\mathbb E[C_n]$, $\mathrm{SD}(C_n)$, $\mathbb E[\widehat{P}_n]$, and $\mathrm{SE}(\widehat{P}_n)$.
2. Calculate all four quantities for $n=25$.
3. Calculate all four quantities for $n=100$.
4. Explain why every realized count is an integer although an expected count need not be.
5. When $n$ is multiplied by four, state the exact factor change in the count center, count standard deviation, proportion center, and proportion standard error.
6. Identify which steps require independence and which expectation steps use linearity without requiring independence.

### Problem 4. Square-root scaling in the fixed `wage1` population

**Class-guide connection:** [Center and spread of a sample mean](../#c11-stop-3) and [Counts and proportions from indicators](../#c11-stop-4)

Treat the 526 historical [`wage1` rows](../data/README.md) as a fixed teaching population. Select rows independently and uniformly with replacement. For recorded hourly wage $W$, use

$$
\begin{aligned}
\mu_W &\approx 5.896103, \\
\sigma_W &\approx 3.689574.
\end{aligned}
$$

These quantities are measured in 1976 dollars per hour. For $H=\mathbf{1}\lbrace W\geq6\rbrace$, use

$$
p=\frac{197}{526}\approx0.3745247.
$$

1. For $n=25,100,400$, state the exact sampling-distribution center of $\overline{W}_n$ and calculate $\mathrm{SE}(\overline{W}_n)$.
2. For the same sample sizes, state the exact center of $\widehat{P}_n$ and calculate $\mathrm{SE}(\widehat{P}_n)$.
3. Verify numerically that multiplying $n$ by four divides each standard error by two. Explain why dividing by four would be incorrect.
4. The seeded Class 11 sample of size 25 has $\overline{w}_{25}=5.5708$. Calculate its realized error relative to the fixed-file mean and distinguish that error from $\mathrm{SE}(\overline{W}_{25})$.
5. The same sample contains 24 distinct rows. Explain why this observation is compatible with 25 independent draws under sampling with replacement.
6. State the units of the sample-mean standard errors and explain why the sample-proportion standard errors are unitless.

You can complete every part from the supplied values. After your attempt, the [Class 11 script](../class-11-sampling-distributions.R) provides an optional reproducibility check.

### Problem 5. Analytic benchmarks and simulation summaries

**Class-guide connection:** [Simulation meets the analytic benchmark](../#c11-stop-5)

The Class 11 script generates 10,000 repeated samples at each $n$. Consider four of its reported standard deviations:

| Statistic | $n$ | Analytic SE | Simulation SD |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Sample mean | 25 | 0.737915 | 0.745032 |
| Sample mean | 400 | 0.184479 | 0.183028 |
| Sample proportion | 25 | 0.096800 | 0.097396 |
| Sample proportion | 400 | 0.024200 | 0.023938 |

1. For each row, calculate simulation SD minus analytic SE.
2. Explain why the analytic SE and simulation SD target the same sampling-distribution spread but need not be numerically identical.
3. Would increasing the number of repetitions from 10,000 to 50,000 change the analytic SE, the sample size inside each repeated sample, or the fixed population? What discrepancy would generally become smaller?
4. Explain why a simulation can check whether the implemented results align with an analytic benchmark but is not a proof of the benchmark.
5. State one distributional-shape claim and one broader-population claim that this table does not establish.

### Problem 6. Audit one sampling-distribution report

**Class-guide connection:** [Audit a sampling-distribution report](../#c11-stop-6)

Audit this exact claim after completing Problems 1–5:

> “The one realized mean 5.5708 is the sampling distribution. Since the analytic standard error for n=400 is 0.184479, every realized sample mean must be within 0.184479 of 5.896103, and the standard error is the error in any one sample. Because the simulated standard deviation 0.183028 differs from 0.184479, the probability model or code must be wrong. Repeated rows show that sampling with replacement failed. The histogram proves an exact Normal distribution, and sampling 400 rows makes the 1976 file describe current workers.”

1. Identify the first error, then audit every sentence using the stated fixed-population, with-replacement model.
2. Rewrite the claim in no more than six sentences. Preserve the supplied numerical facts while repairing object labels, standard-error interpretation, simulation logic, sampling-mechanism logic, shape, and population scope.
3. Support your repair with one analytic calculation, one simulation comparison, and one sampling-design argument.

**Complete non-AI route:** Classify 5.5708 as one realized statistic and describe the sampling distribution as the distribution of the sample-mean rule over repeated samples. Recalculate $3.689574/\sqrt{400}$, separate that sampling-distribution standard deviation from the error in one realized sample, and compare it with 0.183028 as an empirical simulation summary. Return to the meaning of with-replacement sampling to assess duplicates. Then separate center and spread from distributional shape and distinguish the fixed historical population from current workers.

**Optional AI comparison outside the recommended time:** Only after completing the non-AI audit, give an AI system the identical claim below. Do not upload personal work or unrelated files.

```text

Identify the first error and repair every sentence. Classify the statistical
```

## Compact answer checks

Use these only after completing the recommended route. They confirm targets and principal values, not the complete reasoning.

<details>
<summary>Reveal the compact check for Problem 1</summary>

The 500 values form the population distribution; the 16 selected values form one sample distribution; $\overline X_{16}$ is the statistic before sampling; 84.50 is one realized statistic; and the distribution of $\overline X_{16}$ over possible samples is its sampling distribution. Repetition changes the selected rows and realized statistic, not the fixed population quantities, sampling rule, or sampling distribution.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal the compact check for Problem 2</summary>

The four means are 3, 6, 6, and 9, so their probabilities are $1/4,1/2,1/4$. The center is 6, variance is $9/2=4.5$, and standard deviation is $3/\sqrt2\approx2.1213$ tens of dollars. One draw has mean 6 and variance 9.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal the compact check for Problem 3</summary>

For $n=25$, the count has center 9 and SD 2.4, while the proportion has center 0.36 and SE 0.096. For $n=100$, the values are 36, 4.8, 0.36, and 0.048. Multiplying $n$ by four multiplies count center by four and count SD by two, leaves proportion center fixed, and divides proportion SE by two.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal the compact check for Problem 4</summary>

The sample-mean SEs for $n=25,100,400$ are approximately 0.737915, 0.368957, and 0.184479; the proportion SEs are 0.096800, 0.048400, and 0.024200. The realized $n=25$ error is $5.5708-5.896103=-0.325303$, which is not its SE. A duplicate row is permitted by the sampling design.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal the compact check for Problem 5</summary>

Simulation SD minus analytic SE is 0.007117, $-0.001451$, 0.000596, and $-0.000262$. Finite simulation creates Monte Carlo discrepancy; more repetitions do not change the analytic benchmark, repeated-sample size, or population.

</details>

<details>
<summary>Reveal the compact check for Problem 6</summary>

One realized mean is not a sampling distribution; SE is neither a bound nor one realized error; simulation summaries need not equal analytic targets; duplicate rows are permitted; and neither exact shape nor current-worker coverage follows. The transfer has center 50, analytic SE 2, realized error $-3$, and simulation SD 2.06 as an empirical approximation.

</details>

## Common core and optional extensions

**Common core:** Every object distinction, assumption, derivation, calculation, unit check, simulation comparison, and limitation required in Problems 1–6.

**Optional extensions:** Exact enumeration for larger finite teaching populations; full binomial probability calculations; simulation design and Monte Carlo error; sampling without replacement; unequal selection probabilities; cluster dependence; and finite-population corrections. These extensions do not replace the independent with-replacement core.

**Not yet:** Laws of large numbers, central limit theorems, distributional-shape or tail approximations, estimator design and bias, estimated standard errors, confidence intervals, and hypothesis tests belong to later classes. This practice establishes exact center-and-spread identities only.
