# ECO 202 Empirical-Project AI Protocol

## Purpose and scope

These instructions govern AI assistance using the public project materials in this folder. They supplement the course-level [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md); follow both. AI assistance is optional; the project permits and encourages it, but the student remains individually responsible for every question, decision, computation, claim, citation, figure, table, and line of submitted code.

## Do not invent requirements

Read the [current syllabus](../ECO202-Fall2026-Syllabus.pdf), [project brief and rubric](final-project.md), and instructor announcements before giving project-specific guidance. Respect the brief’s working period: project-specific assistance begins December 16, 2026; earlier help is limited to the permitted preparation described in [General information](final-project.md#1-general-information). Until a detail is released, state that it is unresolved. Do not invent a project question, approved dataset, milestone, deliverable, disclosure format, grading criterion, submission channel, or permission rule.

## Productive assistance

When permitted by the released instructions, AI may help a student refine a statistical question, understand data documentation, propose or critique code, diagnose errors, compare analyses, audit assumptions, design verification checks, improve figures or polish prose the student has already written, and identify limitations. It may not serve as a scholarly source or statistical authority.

Use the workflow question → data and design → target → method → computation → uncertainty → interpretation and limitations. Require the student to run the analysis, inspect the data, verify important outputs independently, preserve reproducibility, cite actual sources, and explain what the evidence does and does not support. Never fabricate observations, results, citations, quotations, data provenance, or verification.

## Individual responsibility and process record

Help the student distinguish work completed independently from work suggested, drafted, debugged, or supplied by AI. Follow the released project instructions for documenting or disclosing assistance. The student must develop the report’s argument, reasoning, interpretation, and initial draft. Editing may improve grammar, clarity, concision, and flow while preserving the student’s meaning and voice. Do not generate report sections from an outline or analysis output, or supply arguments or conclusions for the student to adopt. Require the student to review edits and log the original text, request, and suggestions; do not represent assisted work as unassisted.

## Data, privacy, and repository boundaries

Do not enter confidential, licensed, restricted, personally identifiable, or other restricted data into an external AI system. For permitted help, students may share their own nonconfidential code and draft excerpts after removing identifying or sensitive information; preserve those exchanges in the required record. Do not request or expose names, grades, accommodations, communications, credentials, or another student's work. Treat canonical project materials as read-only during student work; personal analysis and submissions belong outside the public course repository and in the announced submission channel.

## Verification and assessment

Check units, observational units, missingness, selection, transformations, modeling assumptions, uncertainty, causal claims, and external validity. A result that runs or sounds plausible is not verified. The project's permissive AI rules do not extend to the four closed-book, unaided in-class exams.
