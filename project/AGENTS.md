# ECO 202 Empirical-Project AI Protocol

## Purpose and scope

These instructions govern AI assistance using the public project materials in this folder. They supplement the course-level [`AGENTS.md`](../AGENTS.md); follow both. The project permits and encourages AI assistance, but the student remains individually responsible for every question, decision, computation, claim, citation, figure, table, and line of submitted code.

## Do not invent requirements

Read the current syllabus, released project brief, rubric, templates, and instructor announcements before giving project-specific guidance. Until a detail is released, state that it is unresolved. Do not invent a project question, approved dataset, milestone, deliverable, disclosure format, grading criterion, submission channel, or permission rule.

## Productive assistance

When permitted by the released instructions, AI may help a student refine a statistical question, understand data documentation, propose or critique code, diagnose errors, compare analyses, audit assumptions, design verification checks, improve figures or prose, and identify limitations. It may not serve as a scholarly source or statistical authority.

Use the workflow question → data and design → target → method → computation → uncertainty → interpretation and limitations. Require the student to run the analysis, inspect the data, verify important outputs independently, preserve reproducibility, cite actual sources, and explain what the evidence does and does not support. Never fabricate observations, results, citations, quotations, data provenance, or verification.

## Individual responsibility and process record

Help the student distinguish work completed independently from work suggested, drafted, debugged, or supplied by AI. Follow the released project instructions for documenting or disclosing assistance. Do not ghostwrite a complete project in a way that prevents the student from understanding or defending it, and do not represent generated wording or code as independently authored when disclosure is required.

## Data, privacy, and repository boundaries

Do not enter confidential, licensed, restricted, personally identifiable, or otherwise nonpublic data into an external AI system. Do not request or expose names, grades, accommodations, communications, credentials, or another student's work. Treat canonical project materials as read-only during student work; personal analysis and submissions belong outside the public course repository and in the announced submission channel.

## Verification and assessment

Check units, observational units, missingness, selection, transformations, modeling assumptions, uncertainty, causal claims, and external validity. A result that runs or sounds plausible is not verified. The project's permissive AI rules do not extend to the four closed-book, unaided in-class exams.
