{$\bar X_{16}$ has a Normal distribution.}

\written{5}{A nonlinear transformation can preserve dependence}
A discrete variable $X$ has distribution
\begin{center}\begin{tabular}{c|ccc}\toprule
$x$ & $-1$ & $0$ & $1$\\\midrule
$\mathbb P(X=x)$ & $1/4$ & $1/4$ & $1/2$\\\bottomrule
\end{tabular}\end{center}
Define $H=X^2$.
\begin{parts}
\item \pts{6} Write the CDF $F_X(t)$ as a piecewise function. Calculate $\mathbb E[X]$ and $\mathrm{Var}(X)$.
\workspace{1.25in}
\item \pts{4} Give the distribution of $H$ and compute its expectation and variance. Express $H$ as the indicator of an event involving $X$.
\workspace{0.95in}
\item \pts{6} Calculate $\mathbb E[XH]$, $\mathrm{Cov}(X,H)$, and $\mathrm{Var}(X+H)$.
\workspace{1.05in}
\item \pts{4} Use one event-probability comparison to show directly that $X$ and $H$ are dependent. Explain why treating their product expectation as the product of expectations would be incorrect.
\workspace{0.9in}
\end{parts}

\written{6}{Estimating a population maximum}
A population variable takes values 0, 2, and 4 with probabilities $1/2$, $1/4$, and $1/4$, respectively. Draw independently with replacement and let $M_2=\max(X_1,X_2)$. The target is the largest possible population value, $m=4$.

You may use $\mathrm{MSE}(T;m)=\mathbb E[(T-m)^2]=\mathrm{Var}(T)+(\mathbb E[T]-m)^2$.
\begin{parts}
\item \pts{6} Find the complete sampling distribution of $M_2$. Explain how the event $M_2\leq2$ helps determine the probabilities.
\workspace{1.05in}
\item \pts{5} Calculate $\mathbb E[M_2]$ and its bias as an estimator of $m$. Explain why the bias has this sign.
\workspace{1in}
\item \pts{5} Calculate $\mathrm{MSE}(M_2;m)$ directly from the sampling distribution. What distinct notion does MSE capture compared with bias alone?
\workspace{1in}
\item \pts{4} For $n$ independent draws, let $M_n$ be their maximum. Derive an exact expression for $\mathbb P(M_n=4)$ and explain why the original two-draw distribution cannot be retained when $n$ changes.
\workspace{1in}
\end{parts}

\written{7}{A discrete count and its continuous approximation}
Suppose $H_1,\ldots,H_{48}$ are independent Bernoulli indicators for a specified household characteristic, each with probability $p=1/4$. Let $K=\sum_{i=1}^{48}H_i$ and $\widehat P=K/48$.

Use $\Phi(z)=\mathbb P(Z\leq z)$ for standard Normal $Z$, with $\Phi(11/6)\approx0.9666$ and $\Phi(5/3)\approx0.9522$.
\begin{parts}
\item \pts{6} Give the exact distribution family and parameters of $K$. Derive the means and standard errors of $K$ and $\widehat P$. Check the expected numbers of successes and failures.
\workspace{1.05in}
\item \pts{6} Use a continuity-corrected Normal approximation to calculate $\mathbb P(K\leq17)$. Show the boundary and standardization. Give the equivalent event for $\widehat P$.
\workspace{1.1in}
\item \pts{4} Extend the model to $n$ indicators. Explain the different limiting behavior described by the LLN for $\widehat P_n$ and by the CLT for $(\widehat P_n-p)/\sqrt{p(1-p)/n}$.
\workspace{1in}
\item \pts{4} A report says, ``Because the expected count is 12, exactly 12 households in this sample must have the characteristic.'' Repair the claim and distinguish the expected count from one realized count.
\workspace{0.85in}
\end{parts}

\written{8}{Combining estimators and reporting uncertainty}
Under one sampling model, $T_1$ is unbiased for $\theta_1$, $T_2$ is unbiased for $\theta_2$, and
\[
\mathrm{Var}(T_1)=4,\qquad \mathrm{Var}(T_2)=9,\qquad
\mathrm{Cov}(T_1,T_2)=1.
\]
You may use $\mathrm{MSE}(T;\theta)=\mathrm{Var}(T)+(\mathbb E[T]-\theta)^2$.
\begin{parts}
\item \pts{5} Show whether $T_1-3T_2$ is unbiased for $\theta_1-3\theta_2$. Does your expectation argument require independence?
\workspace{0.95in}
\item \pts{5} Find the variance and MSE of $T_1-3T_2$ for that target. Explain why simply adding $4+9(9)$ would be incorrect.
\workspace{1in}
\item \pts{5} Is $T_1^2+T_2$ unbiased for $\theta_1^2+\theta_2$? Find its bias and give an unbiased correction. No variance calculation for this nonlinear estimator is required.
\workspace{1.05in}
\item \pts{5} In a separate independent sample of 25 invoices, the observed mean is 80 dollars and sample SD is 10 dollars. Calculate the estimated SE of the mean. An analyst says this establishes that the estimate missed the population mean by exactly that amount. Explain the error and what information is missing.
\workspace{1.05in}
\end{parts}
